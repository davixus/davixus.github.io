---
title: Softball HLP vs. a traditional swing
description: A plain-language comparison of High-Level Pattern hitting and traditional swing instruction.
---

In softball hitting, **HLP** usually means the **High-Level Pattern**, a coaching framework that emphasizes how the body organizes a powerful swing. HLP instruction often focuses on using the ground and rotating the torso, while letting the hands and barrel work in a way that keeps the bat on a useful path. Coaches may use video and drills to help a hitter feel and repeat that movement.

“Traditional” instruction is not one single method. It often gives familiar checkpoints: get balanced, stride toward the pitcher, keep your hands back, and swing through the ball. Those cues can make the swing easier to teach, especially to a beginner, but different coaches use them differently. Some traditional programs also teach rotation, adjust to the hitter, and look much like HLP in practice.

The useful comparison is less about labels and more about results. A hitter needs timing for a fast pitch, a comfortable move into the swing, a barrel that meets the ball, and a safe, repeatable approach. HLP offers one way to explain those goals; traditional cues offer another. Neither name guarantees better hitting. A coach should adapt instruction to the athlete, check whether contact and confidence improve, and avoid forcing every player into an identical swing.

Fastpitch adds its own timing challenge: the ball arrives quickly, and pitchers change speed and location. A player may need a simple rhythm that starts early enough to be ready, then lets her adjust as the pitch comes in. No matter the teaching language, useful practice includes seeing different pitches, making decisions to swing or take, and tracking whether the hitter can make solid contact under pressure. Ask what a cue is meant to help the player do, then judge it by how well it works for that player over time.
